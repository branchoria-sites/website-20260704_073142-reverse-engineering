## Introduction

High-priority export-control lists are increasingly shaped by what investigators actually recover from missiles, drones and other military systems on the battlefield. Rather than relying solely on theoretical assessments of military technology, governments now use evidence from weapon debris to identify the specific electronic components, radio-frequency modules, machine tools and manufacturing inputs that repeatedly appear in operational weapons. The result is a more targeted approach to export controls: items are prioritised because they have been proven to support military production, not merely because they could do so in theory. In the context of [reverse engineering foreign military technology]({{ 'reverse-engineering-foreign-military/' | relative_url }}), [battlefield recovery]({{ 'recovery-teams/' | relative_url }}) has therefore become a mechanism for refining sanctions, directing enforcement resources and identifying supply-chain vulnerabilities that adversaries continue to exploit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/list-common-high-priority-items_en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: finance.ec.europa.eu">[Finance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">finance.ec.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">list common high priority items en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of Common High Priority Items (Version of February 2024)February 22, 2024 — The List of Common High Priority Items is divided...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Battlefield Findings Feed High-Priority Item Lists

The most influential example is the Common High Priority Items List developed by the European Union, United States, United Kingdom, Japan and other partners in response to evidence collected from weapons used against Ukraine. Officials did not begin with a broad catalogue of sensitive technologies and work downward. Instead, investigators documented components recovered from Russian missiles, drones, helicopters and other military systems, then used those findings to determine which categories deserved enhanced scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/list-common-high-priority-items_en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: finance.ec.europa.eu">[europa.eu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">finance.ec.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">list common high priority items en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of Common High Priority Items (Version of February 2024)February 22, 2024 — The List of Common High Priority Items is divided...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2024</span></span></span>

This approach reflects a practical policy problem. Modern weapons often contain thousands of components, many of which have legitimate civilian uses. Governments therefore need a way to distinguish between ordinary commercial trade and items that demonstrably contribute to military capability. Battlefield recovery provides that evidence. When the same integrated circuit, navigation component or radio module repeatedly appears in recovered weapons, it becomes harder to argue that the item is only a hypothetical concern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/list-common-high-priority-items_en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: finance.ec.europa.eu">[Finance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">finance.ec.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">list common high priority items en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of Common High Priority Items (Version of February 2024)February 22, 2024 — The List of Common High Priority Items is divided...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2024</span></span></span>

The resulting lists are intended not only to prohibit exports but also to guide private-sector due diligence. Exporters, distributors, freight forwarders and customs agencies are encouraged to treat listed items as elevated-risk goods requiring additional scrutiny, particularly when shipments involve known transshipment hubs or unusual end users.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bis.gov/licensing/country-guidance/common-high-priority-items-list-chpl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bis.gov">[bis.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bis.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bureau of Industry and SecurityCommon High Priority Items List (CHPL)In this notice, BIS is publicizing these 50 common high-priority ite...</span></span></span>

## Which Parts and Inputs Become Enforcement Priorities

The structure of modern high-priority lists reveals how policymakers interpret battlefield evidence.

### Microelectronics at the Top of the List

Integrated circuits and other microelectronic components occupy the highest priority tier because recovered weapons repeatedly show their importance in guidance, navigation, communications and control functions. Policymakers consider these items especially sensitive because Russia has limited domestic capability to replace many advanced chips and because global manufacturing is concentrated among a relatively small number of producers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bis.gov/licensing/country-guidance/common-high-priority-items-list-chpl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bis.gov">[Bureau of Industry and Security]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bis.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bureau of Industry and SecurityCommon High Priority Items List (CHPL)In this notice, BIS is publicizing these 50 common high-priority ite...</span></span></span>

This creates a policy logic that differs from traditional arms embargoes. Instead of focusing on complete weapons, controls target the specialised components without which precision-guided systems become much harder to manufacture.

### Communications and Navigation Components

A second cluster of priorities consists of radio-frequency transceiver modules, wireless communications equipment, satellite-navigation-related electronics and passive electronic components. These technologies are repeatedly associated with drone control systems, missile guidance functions and battlefield communications. Their presence in recovered systems explains why they receive heightened attention despite extensive civilian demand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/list-common-high-priority-items_en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: finance.ec.europa.eu">[europa.eu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">finance.ec.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">list common high priority items en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of Common High Priority Items (Version of February 2024)February 22, 2024 — The List of Common High Priority Items is divided...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2024</span></span></span>

From an enforcement perspective, these parts are attractive targets because they often pass through international commercial channels before being diverted. Tightening controls around them can therefore disrupt procurement networks without requiring comprehensive restrictions on entire industrial sectors.

### Manufacturing Equipment and Industrial Inputs

Battlefield analysis has also broadened attention beyond finished electronics. Investigators and policymakers increasingly focus on the machinery and production inputs needed to manufacture weapon components. The Common High Priority framework includes categories linked to printed-circuit-board production, advanced manufacturing and precision metalworking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/list-common-high-priority-items_en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: finance.ec.europa.eu">[Finance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">finance.ec.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">list common high priority items en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of Common High Priority Items (Version of February 2024)February 22, 2024 — The List of Common High Priority Items is divided...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2024</span></span></span>

This reflects a growing understanding that military production depends on industrial ecosystems rather than individual chips. Precision machine tools, specialised controllers and advanced manufacturing equipment may never appear inside a missile, yet they can be indispensable to producing missile components at scale.

The importance of this category became particularly visible when investigations highlighted the continued use of Western computer numerical control (CNC) technologies in Russian missile production. Such findings reinforced the view that restricting manufacturing capabilities can be as significant as restricting individual electronic parts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ft.com/content/990bbc2f-6b6f-4990-b022-3bf4cd090686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ft.com">[Financial Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Financial Times Russian producers of Oreshnik supermissile used western tools</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ukrainian intelligence identified two key Russian developers—Moscow Institute for Thermal Technology (MITT) and Sozvezdie—as relying on G...</span></span></span>

## How Governments Turn Debris Into Policy

The path from battlefield fragment to export-control priority typically involves several stages.

Investigators first document components recovered from military systems and identify manufacturers, model numbers and technical specifications. Repeated appearances across different weapon types increase confidence that a component is strategically important rather than incidental. These findings are then compared with trade records, procurement networks and industrial dependencies to determine whether restricting access could meaningfully affect military production.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_Armament_Research" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Conflict Armament Research</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Conflict Armament Research</span></span></span>

Governments subsequently translate technical findings into customs and trade-control categories. Rather than listing every individual component model, they often designate broader Harmonised System (HS) codes that cover entire classes of goods. This allows customs agencies to monitor trade flows more effectively while creating a common enforcement framework across multiple jurisdictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://finance.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/list-common-high-priority-items_en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: finance.ec.europa.eu">[Finance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">finance.ec.europa.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">list common high priority items en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">List of Common High Priority Items (Version of February 2024)February 22, 2024 — The List of Common High Priority Items is divided...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2024</span></span></span>

An important consequence is that export controls become more adaptive. Official guidance explicitly describes the high-priority lists as living [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) that can be updated as new battlefield evidence emerges. The first challenge is that many prioritised items are genuinely dual-use. ## Why Battlefield-Proven Risk Matters More Than Theoretical Risk

The significance of high-priority parts lists lies in their shift from prediction to observation. Traditional export controls often relied on expert judgement about what might support military programmes. Battlefield-driven lists instead focus on what has already been found inside operational weapons.

That distinction has important governance consequences. It provides stronger justification for regulatory action, improves coordination among allied governments and helps direct scarce enforcement resources toward technologies with demonstrated military relevance.
