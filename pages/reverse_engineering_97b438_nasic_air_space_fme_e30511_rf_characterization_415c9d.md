## Introduction

When foreign aircraft, missiles or air-defence systems arrive at facilities such as the National Air and Space Intelligence Center ([NASIC]({{ 'nasic/' | relative_url }})) for exploitation, some of the most valuable information comes not from disassembly but from radio-frequency (RF) testing. Radars, datalinks and missile seekers reveal critical aspects of their behaviour through the [signals]({{ 'signals/' | relative_url }}) they transmit, receive and process. By measuring those signals under controlled conditions, analysts can determine how a system detects targets, resists jamming, communicates with other platforms and guides weapons to impact. RF characterisation turns electronic emissions into intelligence, helping transform a captured device from an unknown threat into a measurable and understandable system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.keysight.com/nl/en/assets/7018-01694/application-notes/5989-7575.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: keysight.com">[Keysight United States]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">keysight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Keysight United StatesRadar MeasurementsLearn radar basics, the fundamentals of measuring basic pulsed radar in radar systems and availab...</span></span></span>

Unlike visual inspection, RF testing exposes operational behaviour. A radar may appear sophisticated externally, yet its pulse patterns, frequency agility, antenna behaviour and signal-processing methods can reveal strengths, [weaknesses]({{ 'weaknesses/' | relative_url }}) and design compromises. ## What RF Characterisation Measures

RF characterisation focuses on how a system behaves electromagnetically rather than how it looks mechanically. Engineers place hardware in controlled test environments and record the signals it emits or responds to. These measurements can reveal the system's operating logic and intended battlefield role.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://micomlabs.com/radar-testing-procedures/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: micomlabs.com">[MiCOM Labs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">micomlabs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MiCOM LabsRadar Testing Procedures: RF Accuracy &amp; ComplianceProvides a controlled environment for measuring unintended emissions. * Operating frequency and frequency-hopping behaviour.
* Pulse repetition frequency (PRF) and pulse timing.
* Pulse width, power output and modulation techniques.
* [Antenna scan patterns]({{ 'scan-patterns/' | relative_url }}) and beam steering behaviour.

### Why Signal Behaviour Often Matters More Than Hardware Layout

Two radars can share similar antennas, processors and packaging while performing very differently. RF testing reveals distinctions that physical inspection alone cannot uncover. Frequency-agile radars, for example, may rapidly shift operating frequencies to complicate interception or jamming. Measuring those shifts provides insight into electronic protection strategies and spectrum-management techniques.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ntrl.ntis.gov/NTRL/dashboard/searchResults/titleDetail/ADA111647.xhtml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntrl.ntis.gov">[NTRL]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntrl.ntis.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">· Radar homing · Radar transmitters · Homing devices · Guided missile trajectories · Terminal homing · Electronic...Read more...</span></span></span>

Likewise, pulse-compression methods, waveform shaping and modulation schemes can indicate how a radar balances range, accuracy and resistance to electronic attack. ## How Emissions Support Countermeasure Design

One of the principal reasons for exploiting foreign RF systems is to improve defensive and offensive electronic warfare capabilities. Once engineers understand how a radar transmits and processes signals, they can assess which techniques are most likely to confuse, deceive or avoid it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.leidos.com/capabilities/cyber/electronic-warfare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: leidos.com">[Leidos]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">leidos.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic WarfareDatalinks Computers (including software algorithms) RF and EO/IR missile seekers. ### Building Realistic Threat Representations

A significant challenge in electronic warfare is reproducing foreign emitters accurately enough for testing and [training]({{ 'training/' | relative_url }}). Measured RF data allows engineers to create representative threat simulations that mimic real radar behaviour rather than simplified approximations. Such simulations can then be used to evaluate aircraft self-protection systems, warning receivers and electronic attack equipment under realistic conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/36720983.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: core.ac.uk">[core.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">core.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">COREElectronic combat hardware-in-the-loop testing in an open</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An open loop simulator provides a one way path from the simulator to the EC system for a representative threat signal to travel.Read more...</span></span></span>
