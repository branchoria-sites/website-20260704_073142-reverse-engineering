## Introduction

Mobile threat simulators are one of the most practical outcomes of studying and [reverse engineering foreign military technology]({{ 'reverse-engineering-foreign-military/' | relative_url }}). Instead of placing rare [captured radars]({{ 'radar-lessons/' | relative_url }}), missile systems or other sensitive equipment on a training range, operators build transportable systems that reproduce the signatures and behaviours that pilots, air-defence crews and electronic-warfare operators actually need to encounter. The result is a repeatable training environment that can be moved between locations, updated as threats evolve and instrumented for detailed performance assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dote.osd.mil/About/Organization/CCM/Warfighter-Training/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dote.osd.mil">[Dote]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dote.osd.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Warfighter TrainingThe HPRTS and PRTS are ruggedized, deployable, ground-based, open-loop radio frequency threat radar simulators. On modern live [training]({{ 'training/' | relative_url }}) ranges, realism depends less on possessing an authentic foreign weapon than on recreating what friendly sensors and crews perceive. Mobile simulators therefore focus on radar emissions, tracking behaviour, engagement sequences, infrared signatures and other observable characteristics. ## Why ranges use mobile fake threats

Training ranges face a practical challenge: real foreign systems are scarce, expensive to maintain and often unsuitable for constant deployment. Even when a military possesses captured equipment, keeping it operational may require unique [spare parts]({{ 'spare-parts/' | relative_url }}), specialist knowledge and strict security controls.

Mobile simulators solve these problems by separating training value from physical authenticity. Instead of recreating every component of an air-defence system, they reproduce the elements that affect aircraft and crews. A pilot's radar warning receiver, for example, must react to realistic search, tracking and engagement emissions. If those cues are reproduced accurately, the training objective can often be achieved without the actual foreign radar being present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.leonardodrs.com/what-we-do/products-and-services/electronic-warfare-ew-threat-simulators/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: leonardodrs.com">[Leonardo DRS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">leonardodrs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Leonardo DRSElectronic Warfare (EW) Threat SimulatorsEW Threat Simulators prepare combat pilots for missile threats. The mobility aspect is equally important. A simulator mounted on trailers, light vehicles or transportable shelters can be repositioned rapidly across a range, creating different threat layouts for successive exercises. This prevents crews from memorising fixed locations and encourages realistic tactical decision-making.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eviden.com/insights/press-releases/the-eviden-developed-arpege-surface-to-air-radar-threat-simulation-system-deployed-during-a-nato-air-force-training-exercise/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eviden.com">[Eviden]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eviden.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Eviden-developed ARPEGE surface-to-air RADAR...Based on Mobile Radar Threat Simulator (MRTS) solutions, the ARPEGE radar threa...</span></span></span>

## Rugged systems built for repeated exercises

Live ranges demand equipment that can survive frequent transport, harsh weather and continuous operation. As a result, modern mobile threat simulators are designed more like field-deployable military equipment than laboratory instruments.

The United States Department of Defense describes systems such as the High-Power Range Threat Simulator (HPRTS) and Portable Range Threat Simulator (PRTS) as ruggedised, deployable, ground-based radio-frequency threat simulators capable of reproducing threat radar [signals]({{ 'signals/' | relative_url }}) and simultaneous engagement scenarios. Their purpose is not merely demonstration but routine operational training and testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dote.osd.mil/About/Organization/CCM/Warfighter-Training/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dote.osd.mil">[Dote]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dote.osd.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Warfighter TrainingThe HPRTS and PRTS are ruggedized, deployable, ground-based, open-loop radio frequency threat radar simulators. The emphasis on ruggedness reflects a simple reality: a range system may participate in hundreds of training events over its lifetime. Reliability and maintainability often matter more than perfect physical replication.

### Mobile deployment as a training multiplier

Mobility allows a relatively small number of simulators to support many different units and locations. Historical US electronic-combat training programmes used mobile deployments specifically to bring threat environments to operational units rather than requiring every unit to travel to a dedicated range. Audit records from the Department of Defense noted the value of mobile simulator assets in creating temporary electronic-combat ranges for aircrew training.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/1992/Jul/15/2001714601/-1/-1/1/92-125.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarDoD Management of Electronic Warfare Threat Simulators...March 9, 2015 — 15 Jul 1992 — At deployed sites, the. 87 EWAS...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2015</span></span></span>

This model remains attractive because it spreads scarce threat-replication resources across a larger force while reducing training bottlenecks.

## Changing scenarios without moving real weapons

The greatest advantage of portable simulators is flexibility. A live range operator can alter an entire air-defence scenario through software and mission planning rather than through large-scale physical movement of equipment.

Modern systems can simulate different radar types, engagement doctrines and force structures. Operators can create the appearance of layered air-defence networks, overlapping radar coverage or coordinated missile engagements. The same hardware may represent several different threat systems across multiple exercises.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sigmadefense.com/capabilities/radar-threat-simulators/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sigmadefense.com">[Sigma Defense]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sigmadefense.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sigma DefenseRadar Threat SimulatorsSigma Defense delivers high-fidelity radar threat simulators, replicating adversary systems &amp; RF envi...</span></span></span>

A recent example is the ARPEGE surface-to-air radar threat simulation system used during NATO Tactical Leadership Programme training at Los Llanos Air Base in Spain. Based on Mobile Radar Threat Simulator technology, the system combines a command post with remote vehicles carrying radar signal generators and tracking antennas. Its architecture allows complex operational scenarios to be tailored to specific air-force training requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eviden.com/insights/press-releases/the-eviden-developed-arpege-surface-to-air-radar-threat-simulation-system-deployed-during-a-nato-air-force-training-exercise/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eviden.com">[eviden.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eviden.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Eviden-developed ARPEGE surface-to-air RADAR...Based on Mobile Radar Threat Simulator (MRTS) solutions, the ARPEGE radar threa...</span></span></span>

### Beyond radar-only simulation

## What mobile simulators contribute to foreign-technology exploitation

Within the broader effort to reverse engineer foreign military technology, mobile threat simulators act as a bridge between intelligence collection and operational readiness. Information extracted from foreign systems becomes usable training content rather than remaining confined to technical reports.

Their value lies in making threat knowledge portable, repeatable and measurable. A realistic threat can be presented to hundreds of crews, adjusted as intelligence changes and evaluated through detailed exercise data. For training ranges, that combination of mobility, adaptability and sustainability is often more useful than relying on a handful of authentic foreign systems that are difficult to operate and impossible to replace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gtri.gatech.edu/newsroom/advanced-radar-threat-system-helps-aircrews-train-evade-enemy-missiles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gtri.gatech.edu">[gatech.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gtri.gatech.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advanced radar threat system helps aircrews train evade enemy missiles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Georgia Tech Research InstituteAdvanced Radar Threat System Helps Aircrews Train to...18 Sept 2023 — The Advanced Radar Threat System Va...</span></span></span>
