## Introduction

Controlled studies of exploded steel pipe bombs occupy an unusual place in metallurgical forensics. Although the devices themselves are not comparable to military munitions in design sophistication, laboratory examinations of their [fragments]({{ 'fragments/' | relative_url }}) have shown that an explosion does not erase all evidence of the metal’s prior condition or of the forces experienced during detonation. Instead, microscopic deformation, wall thinning, grain distortion and hardness changes can survive on recovered fragments and provide measurable clues about how the casing failed. These studies are relevant to [reverse engineering foreign military technology]({{ 'reverse-engineering-foreign-military/' | relative_url }}) because they demonstrate a broader principle: even heavily fragmented debris may retain a record of explosive loading and material response that can be recovered through metallographic analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044580309003933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microstructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — In the United States, pipe bombs...</span></span></span>

## Why Controlled Fragment Studies Matter

One of the most influential experimental programmes in this area was conducted at the University of Rhode Island, where researchers recovered steel pipe fragments after controlled detonations and examined them using optical microscopy, scanning electron microscopy and microhardness testing. Rather than focusing solely on fragment size or blast effects, the work concentrated on what remained inside the metal itself after fracture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044580309003933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[sciencedirect.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microstructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — In the United States, pipe bombs...</span></span></span>

The key finding was that explosive loading left identifiable metallurgical signatures. Researchers observed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Distortion of ferrite grains.
* Deformation of pearlite colonies.
* Mechanical twinning.
* Slip bands and cross-slip bands.
* Permanent changes in hardness.
* Measurable reductions in wall thickness caused by plastic stretching before rupture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/che_facpubs/168/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcommons.uri.edu">[DigitalCommons@URI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcommons.uri.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">che facpubs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DigitalCommons@URIMicrostructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — Recovered pipe bomb fragment...</span></span></span>

</div>

These observations mattered because they showed that the casing did not simply shatter instantaneously. Instead, the metal underwent a short but intense period of plastic deformation before fracture. The resulting microstructure preserved evidence of that process. In forensic reconstruction, this provided a means of inferring relative loading conditions even when explosive residues were absent or degraded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044580309003933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microstructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — In the United States, pipe bombs...</span></span></span>

For analysts examining fragments from foreign military systems, the broader lesson is methodological rather than device-specific. If controlled experiments can correlate microstructural damage with loading history, similar approaches may help interpret recovered missile, artillery or warhead fragments whose manufacturing details are otherwise unknown.

## What Blasts Do Inside Metal Fragments

The most important contribution of these studies is the demonstration that explosive deformation leaves a layered internal record rather than a single uniform damage state.

In recovered steel fragments, researchers found that increasing explosive energy was associated with greater plastic strain, more severe pearlite distortion and higher measured hardness values. The relationship was sufficiently consistent that investigators proposed building reference databases linking fragment microstructure to loading conditions and casing behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044580309003933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[sciencedirect.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microstructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — In the United States, pipe bombs...</span></span></span>

Microscopy revealed that pearlite colonies became elongated and distorted as the pipe wall expanded. Mechanical twins and slip bands appeared within grains, indicating extremely rapid deformation. These are classic indicators of high-strain-rate loading, but the significance lies in their survival after fragmentation. The fracture event did not destroy all traces of the deformation that preceded it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/che_facpubs/168/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcommons.uri.edu">[DigitalCommons@URI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcommons.uri.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">che facpubs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DigitalCommons@URIMicrostructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — Recovered pipe bomb fragment...</span></span></span>

Another notable observation was wall thinning. As internal pressure increased, the steel casing stretched radially before breaking apart. Measurements showed that fragment thickness decreased as loading severity increased, providing a direct geometric indicator of the amount of plastic deformation experienced before rupture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Changes-in-measured-wall-thickness-of-pipe-bomb-fragments-as-a-function-of-filler_fig2_237949096" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Changes in measured wall thickness of pipe bomb...This paper examines the microstructural changes in plain carbon-steel fr...</span></span></span>

For reverse-engineering work, this principle can be valuable because fragment geometry and microstructure together may reveal whether a component failed through brittle fracture, ductile expansion, overstressing or a combination of mechanisms.

## Work Hardening and Radial Hardness Variation

Among the most useful findings from controlled fragment studies was the discovery of systematic hardness changes across individual fragments.

Researchers measured microhardness at different positions through the thickness of recovered steel pipe walls and found a radial hardness gradient. In many cases, the outer wall exhibited higher hardness than the inner wall. This pattern was interpreted as evidence of non-uniform plastic deformation during expansion and fracture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/che_facpubs/168/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcommons.uri.edu">[uri.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcommons.uri.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">che facpubs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DigitalCommons@URIMicrostructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — Recovered pipe bomb fragment...</span></span></span>

The mechanism is work hardening. As metal undergoes severe plastic strain, dislocation density increases and the material becomes harder. Because deformation is not distributed perfectly evenly through the wall thickness, hardness can vary from one region to another. Controlled experiments demonstrated that these variations remained measurable after detonation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/che_facpubs/168/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcommons.uri.edu">[DigitalCommons@URI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcommons.uri.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">che facpubs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DigitalCommons@URIMicrostructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — Recovered pipe bomb fragment...</span></span></span>

Independent work on copper pipe bomb fragments reached a similar conclusion. Post-blast fragments displayed increased Vickers hardness compared with the original pipe material, alongside evidence of wall thinning and plastic strain prior to fracture. The study showed that work hardening can survive even when the fragment has been violently expelled and later exposed to environmental degradation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/physical-characterization-and-recovery-corroded-fingerprint" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ojp.gov">[Office of Justice Programs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">246115 · Journal. Journal of...Read more...</span></span></span>

For military debris analysis, hardness mapping can therefore serve as more than a material-identification tool. It may also help reconstruct how a casing expanded, which regions experienced the greatest strain and whether failure occurred after substantial plastic flow or more abruptly.

## What Fragment Morphology Can and Cannot Reveal

Controlled pipe-bomb experiments also demonstrated links between explosive loading and fragment appearance. Researchers reported differences in fragmentation patterns, fracture-edge geometry and casing thinning under different loading conditions. Some fillers produced relatively large fragments, while others generated more extensive fragmentation and more obvious signs of plastic deformation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/AAFS-2009-A152.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aafs.org">[aafs.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aafs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AAFS 2009 A152</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Criminalistics Section – 2009Pipe bombs are one of the most common types of improvised explosive devices encountered in the United. State...</span></span></span>

However, morphology alone proved insufficient. Similar-looking fragments could arise from different combinations of casing properties and loading conditions. This is why microscopic examination became important. Features such as grain distortion, twinning and hardness gradients supplied evidence that visual inspection alone could not provide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044580309003933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microstructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — In the United States, pipe bombs...</span></span></span>

This distinction carries over to foreign materiel exploitation. Fragment shape may provide initial clues about a weapon, but metallurgical evidence often provides the stronger basis for understanding how the material behaved under explosive stress.

## Limits of Applying Pipe-Bomb Evidence to Military Debris

The usefulness of these studies comes with important limitations. Pipe bombs are typically made from commercially available steels or copper tubing and employ simple cylindrical geometries. Military warheads, missile casings and penetrators often use specialised alloys, controlled [heat treatments]({{ 'heat-treatment/' | relative_url }}), engineered fragmentation schemes and manufacturing processes far removed from those found in improvised devices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044580309003933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microstructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — In the United States, pipe bombs...</span></span></span>

As a result, specific numerical relationships observed in pipe-bomb experiments cannot simply be transferred to military systems. Hardness gradients, wall-thinning behaviour and deformation modes may differ substantially when dealing with maraging steels, high-strength alloy steels, precipitation-hardened alloys or composite structures.

The value of the pipe-bomb literature lies instead in demonstrating persistence of evidence. Controlled experiments repeatedly showed that explosive fragmentation does not necessarily erase microscopic records of deformation. Grain distortion, work hardening, twinning and thickness changes can survive the event and remain available for analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/che_facpubs/168/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitalcommons.uri.edu">[uri.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitalcommons.uri.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">che facpubs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DigitalCommons@URIMicrostructural characterization of pipe bomb fragmentsby O Gregory · 2010 · Cited by 25 — Recovered pipe bomb fragment...</span></span></span>

For analysts reverse engineering foreign military technology, that lesson is more important than any individual pipe-bomb result. Even when only fragments remain, the metal itself may preserve a measurable history of how the component was loaded, expanded and ultimately broken apart.
