## Introduction

In the exploitation of captured foreign military technology, technical [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) often provide a faster route to understanding than the hardware itself. A seized artillery piece, missile launcher or mortar may require extensive inspection, measurement and testing before analysts can estimate its capabilities. A captured firing table, operator handbook or [maintenance]({{ 'maintenance/' | relative_url }}) manual can immediately reveal how the original user expected the system to perform, what variables affected accuracy, which components required adjustment, and what faults crews were trained to recognise. Military technical intelligence doctrine has long treated captured documentation as a core exploitation target alongside physical materiel because documents can rapidly transform an unfamiliar weapon from an unknown object into a structured engineering problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://info.publicintelligence.net/USArmy-DocumentMediaExploitation.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info.publicintelligence.net">[Public Intelligence &#124;]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info.publicintelligence.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USArmy Document Media Exploitation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DOMEX process requires understanding collected, exploited, and processed items. Within the broader question of [captured documents]({{ 'document-haul/' | relative_url }}) versus captured hardware, firing tables and manuals are especially valuable because they sit close to operational reality. Unlike promotional claims or high-level specifications, they are working documents created to help crews employ weapons effectively. That practical focus makes them powerful exploitation shortcuts—but also sources that require careful verification.

## What Firing Tables and Manuals Reveal First

A firing table is a technical reference that links range, ammunition type, environmental conditions and weapon settings to expected projectile behaviour. Field artillery organisations use such tables to convert a desired target effect into firing data. Modern manuals describe similar relationships through digital fire-control systems, but the underlying information remains the same.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://walter.bislins.ch/blog/media/2020-10-23%20Field%20Artillery%20Manual%20Cannon%20Gunnery%20tc3_09x81.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: walter.bislins.ch">[walter.bislins.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">walter.bislins.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2020 10 23 Field Artillery Manual Cannon Gunnery tc3 09x81</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Platoon and Battalion FDC............................ 2-2. Battalion... Chapter 7 described the use of tabular firing tables (TFT) and...</span></span></span>

When captured, these documents can reveal several critical features before any detailed hardware examination begins:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* **Expected performance envelopes.** Range limits, elevation settings, ballistic corrections and ammunition compatibility can indicate how designers expected the weapon to operate.
* **Operational assumptions.** Tables often show which atmospheric variables, terrain factors or calibration procedures mattered enough to require routine adjustment.
* **Ammunition families and variants.** Manuals frequently identify compatible rounds, fuze types and specialised munitions that may not be present with the captured weapon.
* **Crew procedures.** Step-by-step instructions expose how operators were trained to prepare, fire, maintain and troubleshoot equipment.
* **Failure expectations.** Maintenance sections reveal which malfunctions were common enough to deserve dedicated procedures.

</div>

For intelligence analysts, this information creates an immediate framework for understanding a weapon system. Rather than beginning with a completely unknown device, investigators gain access to the original user's conceptual map of the system.

## How Documents Guide Hardware Testing

Captured manuals do not eliminate the need for physical examination, but they can dramatically reduce the time required to organise testing programmes. ### Turning Unknown Equipment into Known Subsystems

A technical manual commonly identifies assemblies, part relationships and maintenance levels. Analysts can use these descriptions to prioritise inspection of critical components instead of [reverse engineering]({{ 'reverse-engineering-foreign-military/' | relative_url }}) every element from scratch.

For example, a maintenance chapter may identify a particular optical sight, servo mechanism or power subsystem as a frequent source of failure. An exploitation team can then concentrate diagnostic effort on those areas rather than distributing resources evenly across the entire system.

### Designing More Efficient Test Programmes

Firing tables provide predicted outputs. Hardware testing can therefore focus on comparing actual behaviour against documented expectations.

Analysts may ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* Does the weapon achieve the stated range?
* Are correction factors accurate?
* Do environmental adjustments work as described?
* Are ammunition compatibility claims supported by physical evidence?

</div>

Because the manual already states expected performance, testing becomes a process of validation and discrepancy analysis rather than open-ended exploration.

### Supporting Translation and Classification

NATO and US technical intelligence doctrine emphasise the role of translators and subject-matter [experts]({{ 'experts/' | relative_url }}) in captured materiel exploitation centres. Technical documents provide terminology, component names and procedural language that help analysts classify equipment correctly and compare it with known foreign systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nllp.jallc.nato.int/cmnt/ciedcoi/CIED%20PUBLICATIONS/Handbooks%20and%20Doctrines/AJP%202.5%28A%29%20Captured%20Persons%2C%20Material%20and%20Documents%20dated%20aug07.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nllp.jallc.nato.int">[NATO JALLC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nllp.jallc.nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATO JALLCAJP-2.5(A) CAPTURED PERSONS, MATERIEL AND...February 20, 2015 — Operations to technically exploit CMAT and ATDs may be conduct...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 20, 2015</span></span></span>

A weapon that initially appears unique may prove to be a modified version of an already catalogued system once terminology and maintenance instructions are translated.

## Historical Evidence of the Shortcut Effect

The value of captured technical documents has been recognised for decades. During and after the Second World War, Allied technical intelligence organisations sought not only enemy weapons but also the manuals, research reports and firing data that explained them. Programmes such as [Operation LUSTY]({{ 'operation-lusty/' | relative_url }}) explicitly combined aircraft recovery with the collection of scientific and technical documentation because documents accelerated understanding of captured technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_LUSTY" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Operation LUSTY</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Operation LUSTY</span></span></span>

The historical record also shows that technical intelligence organisations produced translated foreign firing tables for their own analysts and troops. The former US Army Ordnance Technical Intelligence Agency compiled and distributed translated firing tables for foreign artillery and armoured weapons, demonstrating that captured ballistic data was considered operationally valuable intelligence in its own right.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Ordnance_Technical_Intelligence_Agency" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">United States Army Ordnance Technical Intelligence Agency</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">United States Army Ordnance Technical Intelligence Agency</span></span></span>

Military technical intelligence guidance likewise stresses preserving documents associated with captured equipment and ammunition because exploitation depends on understanding both the physical object and the information accompanying it. Captured enemy ammunition programmes, for example, treat technical exploitation as a means of identifying capabilities, limitations and [countermeasures]({{ 'countermeasures/' | relative_url }}) more rapidly than hardware examination alone would allow.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/policy/army/fm/4-30-13/ch12.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: globalsecurity.org">[Global Security]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">globalsecurity.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Exploitation of CEA and TECHINT...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Where Paper Intelligence Can Mislead

The speed advantage of manuals and firing tables creates a corresponding risk: analysts may assume the documents accurately describe the captured weapon.

In practice, several factors can undermine that assumption.

### Manuals Describe Intent, Not Necessarily Reality

Technical publications often reflect official standards rather than field conditions. A weapon recovered from combat may contain production substitutions, battlefield repairs, worn components or local modifications that never appeared in the original documentation.

A firing table might represent factory-standard ammunition, while the captured stockpile consists of degraded rounds produced under wartime shortages. Hardware testing is necessary to determine whether documented performance remains achievable.

### Documents May Belong to Another Variant

Many weapon families evolve through multiple production blocks and upgrade packages. A captured manual may correspond to an earlier or later version than the equipment actually recovered.

Without careful comparison, analysts can misidentify capabilities, ranges or maintenance procedures.

### Training Material Can Reflect Doctrine Rather Than Capability

Operator manuals frequently describe ideal employment methods. They show how crews are expected to use a weapon, not necessarily how effectively they can do so under combat conditions.

A system may possess impressive documented accuracy but depend on survey procedures, meteorological inputs or maintenance standards that are rarely achieved in practice.

### Deliberate Deception Is Possible

Although less common than simple obsolescence or mismatch, captured documentation can contain misleading information. Intelligence organisations therefore treat documents as evidence to be corroborated rather than accepted uncritically. ## Why Exploitation Teams Still Prioritise Manuals

Despite their limitations, captured firing tables and technical manuals remain among the highest-value documentary finds associated with foreign weapons systems. They compress months of investigative effort into a form already organised by the original designers, maintainers and operators.

A recovered weapon can reveal what exists. A recovered manual can reveal how the system was expected to function, how it was maintained, which performance standards mattered and what limitations its own users anticipated.
